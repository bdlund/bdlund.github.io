BookTok Boom and Public Libraries: A Thematic Analysis of ‘Fangirl Comments on TikTok, Berrypicking Behavior, and Implications for Public Library Services

Zoë Abbie Teel, Brady D. Lund, Ting Wang

Abstract

Recent attention has been drawn to BookTok, a phenomenon emerging within the TikTok social media platform where users actively engage and exchange information relating to popular books. This study examines five distinct TikTok videos tagged with the hashtag #BookTok to explore the types of interactions and information shared in user comments and to uncover the underlying behaviors of fangirls and fandoms. The first 50 comments (those receiving the most interactions) on each of the five videos were analyzed through thematic analysis, with Bates’ Berrypicking Model serving as the theoretical framework. Findings reveal that the most common types of information shared are generic posts providing general information, expressions of emotional impact or attachment to books, and comments aimed at fostering peer interaction. Importantly, this study also considers how these behaviors and communication patterns can inform public library services. Public librarians can interpret BookTok’s dynamics as indicators of reader motivation, emotional connection, and community formation – elements that can enhance collection development, reader’s advisory, and programming. The results demonstrate that the same emotional and social drivers that sustain BookTok communities can be harnessed by libraries to foster engagement, promote collections, and build inclusive spaces for readers across demographics.
