Generative artificial intelligence (AI) policy in higher education: A content analysis of ethical themes in the QS Top 200 university policies (2026)

Brady D. Lund, Bavya Sri Vemulapalli, Ting Wang

Educational Management and Policy, 1(2), 127–139 (2026)

Data bias, privacy, and security appear most often. Explainability, misinformation, and deepfakes are underrepresented. Abstract

The rapid expansion of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) has prompted universities worldwide to develop institutional policies governing its ethical, pedagogical, and administrative use. While global organizations such as UNESCO and national consortia like the Russell Group have issued high-level principles, universities have responded unevenly, producing policies that vary widely in scope, depth, and ethical coverage. Existing studies have examined subsets of institutions—such as the top 50 global universities, U.S. universities, or leading Asian universities—and consistently report gaps in transparency, data governance, academic integrity guidance, and algorithmic literacy. However, no study, to the researchers’ knowledge, has systematically analyzed AI policies across the world’s top 200 universities. This research addresses that gap in policy awareness by conducting a comprehensive content analysis of 197 publicly available AI policies from the 2025 QS Top 200 universities. Using a 16-theme AI ethics coding framework and statistical comparisons across region, size, public/private status, and ranking quartile, the study identifies dominant themes (e.g., data bias, privacy, security), underrepresented areas (e.g., explainability, misinformation, deepfakes), and structural differences in policy emphasis across institutional types. The findings reveal a global policy landscape that is expanding rapidly but remains fragmented, with significant inconsistencies in ethical coverage, documentation expectations, and support for instructors and students. This report contributes a global, data-driven understanding of how universities are governing GenAI and highlights critical gaps that must be addressed to ensure responsible and equitable AI integration in higher education.
