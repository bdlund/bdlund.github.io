Author manuscript

Perceptions of the Fourth Industrial Revolution and AI’s Impact on Society

Brady D. Lund, Daniel Agbaji, Nishith Reddy Mannuru

Perspectives on Global Development and Technology, 23(5-6) (2024)

Download PDFPublished version (DOI)

Key findings

Semi-structured interviews with participants grouped by their exposure to information flows, analyzed thematically, show how people understand AI. Participants defined it with terms such as machine learning, automation, and replication of human intelligence. They saw benefits in problem-solving and efficiency, and raised concerns about job displacement, misinformation, data privacy, and copyright. Their views on government ranged from regulatory oversight to support for development. The authors conclude that AI literacy initiatives should be tailored to different groups and that policy should balance innovation with social accountability.

Abstract

Purpose. This study investigates public perceptions of Artificial Intelligence (AI) within the broader context of the Fourth Industrial Revolution. It focuses on how individuals from different information environments perceive the benefits, risks, and ethical challenges posed by AI technologies – particularly text-generative tools like ChatGPT. Design. Data were collected through semi-structured interviews with participants categorized by information flow exposure. Thematic analysis was applied to identify patterns in perceptions, concerns, and suggested governance approaches related to AI and automation. Findings. Participants commonly defined AI using terms such as "machine learning," "automation," and "human intelligence replication." While acknowledging benefits like problemsolving and increased efficiency, concerns were raised about job displacement, misinformation, data privacy, and copyright. Participants expressed diverse views on the role of government, ranging from regulatory oversight to development support. Originality/value. This study contributes to the understanding of public sentiment toward emerging technologies in the Fourth Industrial Revolution. It highlights the importance of tailored AI literacy initiatives and informs policy frameworks that balance innovation with social accountability.
