Thresholds for survey research sample size: Recommended minimum sample sizes by statistical test and journal type

Brady D. Lund

Teaching and research resource

Download PDFTable as CSV

What the resource provides

The resource reports descriptive statistics (mean, median, quartiles, minimum, and maximum) for sample sizes in published survey studies, separately for SSCI-indexed journals, non-SSCI journals, and all journals combined. From these it recommends minimum sample sizes for studies reporting only descriptive statistics, studies using t-tests or chi-square tests, studies using regression or structural equation modeling, and studies using other tests. Each threshold is the nearest multiple of 50 to the relevant median. Researchers, students, and reviewers can use it to justify a survey's sample size.

Abstract

This resource gives researchers practical minimum sample size targets for questionnaire-based survey research. It reports descriptive statistics (mean, median, quartiles, minimum and maximum) for the sample sizes used in published survey studies, separately for journals indexed in the Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI), non-SSCI journals, and all journals combined. From these distributions it derives recommended minimum targets: a general suggestion, a threshold for studies presenting only descriptive statistics, and thresholds for studies using t-tests or chi-square tests, regression or structural equation modeling (SEM), and other statistical tests. Recommended thresholds are the nearest multiple of 50 to the relevant median. The tables are adapted from Lund (2023), which describes the methodology. The resource is intended for researchers, students, and reviewers who need to justify how many survey respondents are enough.
