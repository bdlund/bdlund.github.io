Privacy and data literacy

This line of work measures and conceptualizes the skills people need to understand and manage their personal data, and examines how well library professionals are prepared to protect patron privacy.

The PDPL Metric is a validated scale for personal data privacy literacy among university students. A companion study applies the concept to academic library employees and finds a gap between how much they think they know and how they score. A critical review traces how data literacy has been defined and assessed across fields since 2000.

PDPL metric: validating a scale to measure personal data privacy literacy among university students

Brady D. Lund, Nathan Brown, Ana Roeschley, Gahangir Hossain · 2026 · Behaviour & Information Technology · Original manuscript

The paper introduces and validates the PDPL Metric, a 24-item scale for measuring personal data privacy literacy among university students. It covers six constructs: perceived risk of data misuse, expectations of informed consent, general privacy concern, privacy management awareness, acceptance of the privacy-utility trade-off, and perceived importance of data security. Each construct was internally consistent, and the six combine into one overall PDPL construct. Scores did not differ by academic level or gender, but did differ between domestic and international students.

Personal data privacy literacy and library privacy awareness and concern among academic library employees

Bryan Anderson, Brady D. Lund, Yara Mohammed, Zafar Imam Khan · 2026 · The Journal of Academic Librarianship · Preprint (submitted manuscript)

A survey of 153 academic library employees found that personal data privacy literacy, self-reported privacy knowledge, and library privacy awareness are related but distinct. Self-reported knowledge tracked library privacy awareness but not measured privacy literacy, which suggests library professionals may overestimate their privacy competence. MLIS education was linked to higher library privacy awareness but not to higher privacy literacy. The most common concerns were external tracking of patron interactions and patrons' limited data privacy literacy, which reflect the risks of vendor-managed digital platforms.

Discourses of data literacy: A critical literature review

Brady D. Lund, Amanda Hovious, Jeonghyun Kim · 2026 · Journal of the Association for Information Science and Technology · Preprint (submitted version)

This critical review draws on literature from many fields published between 2000 and 2025 to examine how data literacy is conceptualized, framed, and assessed. It traces how the concept has developed and analyzes the theoretical frameworks behind it, including competency-based models, critical theory approaches, and learning-centered perspectives. It also identifies gaps in current understanding and areas for future research.
