Technology, methods, and teaching resources

Resources in this group are written for readers who need an accessible starting point or a practical reference: an introduction to an emerging technology and two aids for teaching and planning research.

The quantum computing entry explains the basic concepts and applications for general readers, and a paper on blockchain in higher education uses the NIST Cybersecurity Framework to examine security. The survey sample size tables give recommended minimums by type of statistical test, based on published studies. The ethnography figure shows one possible sequence of steps in an ethnographic project. The tables and figure are released under a CC BY 4.0 license.

Papers and resources

Blockchain Applications in Higher Education Based on the NIST Cybersecurity Framework

Brady D. Lund · 2024 · Journal of Cybersecurity Education, Research and Practice · Author manuscript (CC BY 4.0)

This paper examines how blockchain technology can be integrated into core systems at colleges and universities, using the NIST Cybersecurity Framework as a guide. It defines key terms, including blockchain, consensus mechanisms, decentralized identity, and smart contracts, and looks at how secure blockchain applies to enrollment management, degree auditing, and award processing. Each part of the NIST Framework is used to explore integration and to address persistent security concerns, and the paper identifies blockchain applications and opportunities in the education sector.

Quantum Computing: A Concise Introduction

Brady D. Lund, Sakib Shahriar · 2025 · Encyclopedia · Original manuscript (CC BY 4.0)

This encyclopedia entry introduces quantum computing for general readers. It explains how qubits differ from classical bits and how superposition, entanglement, and quantum tunneling allow new ways of processing information. It discusses potential applications in cryptography, information retrieval, optimization, and artificial intelligence, and describes Grover's and Shor's algorithms. It notes that the technology is still at an early stage.

Thresholds for survey research sample size: Recommended minimum sample sizes by statistical test and journal type

Brady D. Lund · 2026 · Resource (CC BY 4.0)

The resource reports descriptive statistics (mean, median, quartiles, minimum, and maximum) for sample sizes in published survey studies, separately for SSCI-indexed journals, non-SSCI journals, and all journals combined. From these it recommends minimum sample sizes for studies reporting only descriptive statistics, studies using t-tests or chi-square tests, studies using regression or structural equation modeling, and studies using other tests. Each threshold is the nearest multiple of 50 to the relevant median. Researchers, students, and reviewers can use it to justify a survey's sample size.

Ethnographic research: Example of a possible process

Brady D. Lund · 2025 · Figure (CC BY 4.0)

The figure shows one possible sequence for ethnographic research. The researcher starts with a distinct population, selects a subset for naturalistic observation and interaction, and interprets what is observed by asking what the behavior means and why participants value it. The work then narrows to interviews with a smaller group and to archival research. It was made for teaching research methods.
